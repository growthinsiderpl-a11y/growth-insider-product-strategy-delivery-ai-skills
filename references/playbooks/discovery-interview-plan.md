# Discovery Interview Plan

Design a decision-oriented interview plan that uncovers behavior,
context, stakes, and workarounds without drifting into future-
feature speculation.

## When To Run It

- a product problem needs richer qualitative evidence
- the team wants to hear from users before shaping a solution
- current data shows what is happening but not why
- stakeholders are asking for stories and evidence, not generic
  personas

## Do Not Run It When

- the team only needs a usability test of a near-final flow
- the goal is acquisition message testing
- participants are being chosen only for convenience with no
  behavioral relevance
- the research will have no synthesis or decision owner

## Inputs

- decision to inform
- participant profile
- known evidence and unknowns
- constraints on time or access
- interview count and timeline
- capture method

## Procedure

### Define Participant Logic

- recruit by recent behavior, role, and workflow context
- include both successful and struggling users when useful
- state exclusion rules clearly
- protect against sampling only power users
- document what segment coverage will remain missing

### Write Learning Goals

- limit the guide to the questions that could change the decision
- sort goals into context, triggers, current workflow,
  consequences, and prior alternatives
- mark what must be evidenced directly versus triangulated later
- remove nice-to-know questions
- turn stakeholder curiosity into optional probes only if time
  remains

### Draft the Guide

- open with recent real events
- probe for sequence, actors, tools, and decision points
- ask for examples of last time rather than generalized
  preferences
- leave solution ideas to the end if needed
- write follow-up prompts for ambiguity

### Plan Synthesis

- decide how notes and quotes will be tagged
- set a synthesis session close to the interviews
- prepare buckets for patterns, contradictions, surprises, and
  evidence gaps
- define the output artifact before talking to anyone
- ensure synthesis links back to the decision owner

### Protect Quality

- pilot the guide once if possible
- avoid leading phrasing
- do not let stakeholders turn the session into a sales call
- capture confidence and limitations in the final readout
- state what the interview method cannot prove

## Decision Tests

- each interview question maps to a decision-relevant unknown
- the plan favors behavior over opinion
- synthesis is part of the plan, not an afterthought
- participants reflect the workflow under study
- the final readout format is known before recruiting starts

## Outputs

- participant screener
- interview guide
- synthesis template
- decision-oriented readout outline
- evidence gap note for non-covered segments

## Failure Modes

- asking users to design the product
- overloading the interview with too many questions
- collecting quotes without structured synthesis
- treating convenience samples as representative

## Review and Follow-through

- feed findings into the opportunity or assumption map
- schedule usability work if solution questions remain
- close the loop with the decision owner
- store reusable probe wording only when it improves future
  interviews

## Escalate or Stop When

- recruiting criteria drift into convenience rather than behavior
- stakeholders insist on leading users toward the preferred
  solution
- the guide keeps growing even though the decision remains narrow
- synthesis ownership is still undefined the day before interviews

## Worked Scenario

- For a stalled approval workflow, recruit recent failed setups,
  successful admins, and approvers who ignored invites.
- Ask about the last real case, not general preferences, and
  prepare a synthesis board that clusters confusion, delay, and
  trust issues separately.
- That structure prevents the readout from collapsing into generic
  persona language.
