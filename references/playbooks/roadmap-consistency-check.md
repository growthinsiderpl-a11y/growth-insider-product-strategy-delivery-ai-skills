# Roadmap Consistency Check

Check whether a roadmap is internally coherent, outcome-linked,
capacity-aware, and compatible with known dependencies and
constraints.

## When To Run It

- a roadmap is about to be reviewed with leadership
- the roadmap has grown through accretion
- stakeholders sense contradiction but cannot name it
- the team needs to separate commitments from exploration

## Do Not Run It When

- the real need is to create the roadmap from scratch
- the roadmap is actually a delivery schedule owned by
  engineering detail
- there is no objective or strategy context at all
- the user only wants prettier roadmap wording

## Inputs

- roadmap themes
- items and sequencing
- objective links
- metric links
- dependencies
- capacity assumptions

## Procedure

### Inventory the Roadmap

- list items by theme and time horizon
- mark committed versus exploratory work
- attach owner and decision source
- note missing objective or metric links
- identify duplicated items under different names

### Check Alignment

- verify each item maps to a strategy, objective, or risk need
- flag orphan items with no clear why
- look for conflicting themes that chase opposite behaviors
- ensure non-goals are compatible with the roadmap story
- surface items surviving mainly through habit or politics

### Inspect Sequencing

- trace dependencies at the product level
- check if enablers come before dependent commitments
- spot overloaded windows or releases
- flag items that cannot reasonably coexist under current
  capacity
- look for sequencing that delays learning unnecessarily

### Review Decision Integrity

- confirm the roadmap communicates confidence level honestly
- separate hypotheses from promises
- surface what was deferred or removed
- prepare a short list of contradictions to resolve
- ensure stakeholders can tell what is most likely to change next

### Publish Findings

- classify issues by severity
- recommend retire, reorder, narrow, or clarify actions
- link fixes to stakeholder narrative
- recheck after revisions
- preserve a before-and-after trace for major roadmap cleanups

## Decision Tests

- every major item has a purpose
- dependencies are acknowledged
- speculative work is not disguised as committed work
- capacity overreach is surfaced
- contradictions are specific enough to act on

## Outputs

- roadmap inconsistency report
- proposed sequencing changes
- orphan-item list
- stakeholder narrative notes
- confidence labeling recommendations

## Failure Modes

- treating roadmap polish as strategy
- hiding contradictions in ambiguous wording
- publishing too much committed work to appear ambitious
- ignoring dependency debt because it is uncomfortable

## Review and Follow-through

- validate again after major edits
- reuse findings in board and stakeholder updates
- carry unresolved risks into release planning
- review whether roadmap cleanup changes portfolio allocation

## Escalate or Stop When

- the roadmap claims confidence but most items are still
  discovery
- dependencies point to work that has no committed owner
- release windows hold more major items than the team can
  validate safely
- several items survive without any metric linkage

## Worked Scenario

- A quarter includes premium launch, workflow migration,
  analytics cleanup, and two exploratory AI bets.
- Roadmap consistency review may recommend moving the AI bets
  into an explore lane and narrowing the quarter around governed
  approvals plus migration safety.
- That produces a roadmap people can actually reason about.
