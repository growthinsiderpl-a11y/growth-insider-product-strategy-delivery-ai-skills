# Board Update Narrative

Prepare a concise board or executive update that explains product
performance, choices, risks, and next decisions without hiding
uncertainty.

## When To Run It

- a board meeting or executive review is approaching
- major strategy or roadmap changes need explanation
- leadership needs a clear product narrative
- product wants to show disciplined decision-making rather than
  activity volume

## Do Not Run It When

- the artifact is for public marketing use
- there is no decision or ask in the update
- the team wants to exaggerate confidence beyond the evidence
- the update is expected to stand in for missing product strategy

## Inputs

- meeting objective
- key evidence
- metrics
- major decisions
- risks
- ask or recommendation

## Procedure

### Frame the Narrative

- name the meeting context and the product question
- choose the key storyline: performance, strategic pivot,
  learning, risk, or resource need
- state the ask early
- remove operational noise that does not support the decision
- decide what one message should still be remembered after the
  meeting

### Curate Evidence

- select only the metrics and customer evidence that clarify the
  story
- show trend and limitation together
- include the trade-off or alternative considered
- mark what remains uncertain
- keep evidence proportionate to the board question rather than
  the team effort

### Write the Recommendation

- state the proposed move in plain language
- include why it is appropriate under current constraints
- show what will not be done
- prepare one sentence on what would change the recommendation
- connect the recommendation to business and product consequences

### Prepare Objections

- anticipate finance, sales, or delivery concerns
- write evidence-backed responses
- avoid defensive language
- keep open questions visible if they matter to the board decision
- show where additional evidence will arrive later

### Close the Update

- capture decisions made in the meeting
- identify the next review signal
- document the requested decision or endorsement
- keep the artifact reviewable in a short meeting
- prepare any follow-up packet without expanding the main story

## Decision Tests

- the update includes a clear ask
- evidence supports the story
- trade-offs are visible
- uncertainty is not hidden
- the narrative can survive a short meeting

## Outputs

- board memo
- narrative outline
- objection handling notes
- follow-up decision tracker
- version suitable for stakeholder recap

## Failure Modes

- turning the board update into a product status dump
- overselling certainty
- omitting the trade-off or ask
- burying the critical message in appendix-like detail

## Review and Follow-through

- capture decisions made in the meeting
- update roadmap or portfolio artifacts accordingly
- reuse only if assumptions remain current
- log which questions the board still needs answered

## Escalate or Stop When

- the update has no ask
- the narrative depends on hiding changed assumptions
- metrics are included only because they exist, not because they
  explain the decision
- the team cannot articulate what would change the recommendation

## Worked Scenario

- A board memo can explain a delayed premium launch by showing
  stronger fit evidence in governed accounts and a safer staged
  release plan.
- That is more credible than a generic statement about complexity
  or team bandwidth.
