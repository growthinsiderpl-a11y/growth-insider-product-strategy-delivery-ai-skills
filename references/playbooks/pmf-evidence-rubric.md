# PMF Evidence Rubric

Assess whether the available evidence justifies a narrow product-
market-fit claim without inventing an opaque PMF score.

## When To Run It

- leaders are asking whether the product has fit in a segment
- retention and anecdotal enthusiasm appear to conflict
- the team wants a disciplined evidence review before scaling
- pricing or acquisition plans are about to depend on a fit claim

## Do Not Run It When

- the user wants a single score or maturity badge
- evidence is almost entirely acquisition data
- the question is about technical performance rather than customer
  fit
- the segment or use case is not defined

## Inputs

- segment definition
- usage evidence
- retention evidence
- qualitative evidence
- monetization signals
- known limitations

## Procedure

### Define the Fit Claim

- state the segment, job, and context
- write the specific value proposition under review
- set the confidence language you can defend
- prevent generic market-wide claims
- note what conditions make the claim fragile

### Review Evidence Classes

- check behavior, retention, willingness to continue, and
  replacement of alternatives
- look for direct customer consequences when the product fails
- capture expansion or advocacy signals without over-weighting
  them
- record where instrumentation is missing
- surface evidence that contradicts the dominant narrative

### Inspect Contradictions

- note where qualitative praise conflicts with actual repeat use
- check whether renewals come from switching cost rather than
  value
- identify segment outliers
- look for heavy-service or custom-work caveats
- mark which contradictions must be resolved before scaling

### Judge Sufficiency

- decide whether the evidence supports deepen, narrow, reposition,
  or pause scaling
- write what proof is still missing
- avoid stretching the claim beyond the evidence boundary
- make the review useful for a real decision
- show what would count as disconfirming evidence next quarter

### Publish the Result

- summarize evidence and limitations in plain language
- highlight the next highest-value evidence to collect
- link the conclusion to roadmap or go-to-market implications
- store the artifact for later comparison
- ensure stakeholders cannot mistake the memo for a universal
  score

## Decision Tests

- the output makes a claim, not a score
- limitations stay visible
- behavior and retention evidence are central
- segment boundaries are explicit
- scaling implications are tied to evidence quality

## Outputs

- PMF evidence memo
- fit claim statement
- evidence gap checklist
- recommended next move
- confidence language approved for leadership use

## Failure Modes

- using survey enthusiasm as a stand-in for fit
- declaring fit from top-line revenue growth
- burying the segment limitation
- ignoring the service wrapper around apparent product demand

## Review and Follow-through

- re-run after a meaningful evidence cycle
- use the memo in roadmap and board discussions
- avoid publishing broader claims until stronger evidence exists
- tie each new learning cycle back to the same claim language

## Escalate or Stop When

- the fit claim is broad enough to hide segment differences
- revenue narratives rely on service customization more than
  product behavior
- advocacy is strong but repeat use is inconsistent
- support load is rising in the very segment being called the fit
  winner

## Worked Scenario

- If logistics teams renew and expand while lighter service teams
  churn after setup, the evidence rubric should support a narrow
  fit claim for governed multi-role workflows only.
- That conclusion should then shape roadmap, packaging, and sales
  language rather than producing a comfort score.
