# Product-Market Fit and Customer Evidence

Use this hub to reason about product-market fit as evidence quality, repeat behavior, and segment fit rather than as a single magic label.

## Purpose

This hub helps the team make narrow, defensible fit claims, expose where the product is genuinely solving a recurring problem, and decide whether to deepen, narrow, reposition, or pause scaling.

## What This Hub Owns

- Evidence-based product-market fit discussion.
- Segment-specific value proof.
- Customer evidence logging and interpretation.
- Renewal, expansion, and advocacy signals tied to product behavior.
- Decision rules for deepen, narrow, reposition, or pause.
- Clarity on what a fit claim actually means and where it stops.

## Route Away When

- The request is for a fake product-market fit label or maturity badge.
- The analysis is brand awareness with no product usage evidence.
- The problem is technical performance detached from customer outcomes.
- The team wants persona theater instead of observed behavior.
- The communication goal is to smooth over fit limitations for optics.

## Core Questions

- For which segment and workflow does the product create repeatable value?
- What behavior proves the product is solving a recurring problem?
- Which customers retain because of real value and which retain because switching is painful?
- What qualitative evidence supports the quantitative retention story?
- Which important signal is still missing before the team should claim fit?
- What would count as evidence of misfit even if revenue is still growing?

## Evidence To Gather

- Retention by segment, role, use case, or workflow.
- Activation completion tied to a real value moment rather than to superficial setup.
- Expansion, downgrade, renewal, and cancellation reasons.
- Evidence that the product replaces a prior workflow, tool, or manual process.
- Customer behavior when the product is unavailable or fails in a critical moment.
- Contradictory evidence, such as heavy service rescue, exception handling, or support burden.

## Decision Procedure

### Define the Fit Claim Narrowly

- Name the segment, job, and context explicitly.
- State the claimed value outcome in plain language.
- Attach observed behavior that supports the claim.
- Label the claim partial if it applies only to one slice of the customer base.
- State what is outside the claim boundary.

### Review Evidence Classes

- Use qualitative and quantitative evidence together.
- Check whether retention, depth of usage, renewal behavior, and user testimony tell a consistent story.
- Treat expansion and advocacy as supporting signals, not as substitutes for repeat value.
- Prefer longitudinal evidence over one-time snapshots.
- Keep limitations visible, including pricing immaturity, service wrappers, or data gaps.

### Interpret Contradictions

- Separate healthy repeated use from forced compliance or contractual stickiness.
- Look for concentration risk where only one segment or workflow shows strong value.
- Inspect where support burden, customization, or founder intervention props up retention.
- Distinguish product fit improvement from distribution improvement.
- Write down what evidence would disconfirm the current fit claim next.

### Choose the Next Move

- Deepen where evidence is strongest and the product already earns repeat use.
- Narrow when only one segment shows durable value.
- Reposition when acquisition demand and retention behavior disagree.
- Pause aggressive scaling when the commercial engine is outrunning product proof.
- Carry the fit claim forward into roadmap, packaging, and stakeholder language.

## Failure Modes

- Declaring fit because signups are rising.
- Confusing service revenue with product pull.
- Using enthusiasm surveys alone as fit proof.
- Averaging segment behavior until the differences disappear.
- Overfitting to one lighthouse customer.
- Treating one quarter of good sales as durable fit.

## Cross-Skill Handoffs

- Hand narrative amplification and market messaging to marketing after the fit claim is defined honestly.
- Hand systems reliability or performance root-cause work to engineering when they materially affect the customer evidence.
- Keep the fit claim, segment logic, and evidence interpretation inside the product boundary.
- Share the limitations of the claim with leadership and revenue teams so they do not overextend it.

## Expected Outputs

- Product-market fit evidence memo.
- Segment fit comparison.
- Evidence gap register.
- Reposition, narrow, or deepen recommendation.
- Review plan with explicit confidence language.
- Board-safe fit statement with boundaries.

## Example Prompts

- Assess whether our workflow product has real fit in mid-market governed teams.
- We have strong activation but weak month-two retention. Does that support a fit claim?
- Rewrite our product-market fit story as evidence rather than founder belief.

## Decision Rules

- A fit claim must name the segment and use case, or it is too broad to trust.
- Retention supported by manual service rescue counts differently from product-native retention.
- If usage is deep but expansion is absent, inspect procurement constraints, not only product value.
- If revenue is growing while the natural repeat behavior is weak, do not describe that as strong fit.
- After partial fit, the usual next move is deeper value in the winning segment rather than broader market spread.
- A lighthouse customer can prove the product is possible without proving that the segment is repeatable.

## Worked Example

- A workflow product retains logistics teams with complex multi-role approvals but loses lighter-use teams after initial setup.
- The evidence memo should avoid saying the product has broad product-market fit across all operations teams.
- A narrower and more accurate claim is that the product shows durable fit in governed, exception-heavy workflows where auditability and role clarity matter.
- That claim changes roadmap logic toward governance depth, risk-state clarity, and permission confidence rather than broad cosmetic adoption work.
- It also changes go-to-market posture because the team should deepen the winning segment before expanding the story.

## What The Final Fit Statement Must Contain

- The segment and workflow where fit appears strongest, plus the adjacent territory where the claim does not yet hold.
- Evidence that users return for value rather than because migration, contracts, or service effort keep them trapped.
- The operational or product risks that could break the fit claim even if revenue remains temporarily healthy.
- The next evidence milestone required before sales, leadership, or roadmap language becomes broader.
